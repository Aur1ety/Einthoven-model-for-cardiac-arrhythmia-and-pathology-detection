Einthoven Project Analysis

Einthoven: Physically Grounded Deep Learning for ECG Diagnosis

What is this project?

The one-line version: an ECG machine gives you 12 separate traces. Under the standard heart-vector (dipole) approximation, those traces are projections of one 3D electrical vector that changes through each heartbeat. This project tests whether giving a neural network that reconstructed 3D vector (the vectorcardiogram, or VCG) on top of the 12 raw traces makes it better at diagnosing heart conditions.

The hypothesis: modern deep learning models treat the 12 leads as a flat pile of 12 signals and learn the relationships between them themselves. But a well-known estimate exists: Kors et al. (1990) published a fixed linear regression that reconstructs the 3D cardiac vector from leads I, II and V1 to V6. If we hand the model that reconstruction directly as 3 extra channels, maybe it learns faster and diagnoses better. The honest question is whether it already figures all of that out on its own.

Short answer (spoiler): under our exact protocol, the 3 physics channels added no measurable improvement. That is a real, defensible negative result, and the project produced side findings that stand on their own: a quality-control audit of the lead identities in both of PTB-XL's file sets (100 Hz and 500 Hz), and a check that lets the two file sets test each other.

The data

Primary dataset: PTB-XL (Wagner et al. 2020, Scientific Data)

  • 21,799 real 12-lead ECG recordings from 18,869 patients, 10 seconds each, stored at 500 Hz with a downsampled 100 Hz copy (the models use the 100 Hz files)
  • Labels are SCP-ECG statements. 16,056 records, including all of folds 9 and 10 (validation and test), were validated by a cardiologist; the other 5,743 are automatically generated reports
  • Labels are grouped into 5 diagnostic superclasses:
    • NORM (normal), MI (myocardial infarction or injury), STTC (ST/T changes), CD (conduction disturbances), HYP (hypertrophy)
  • 411 records are excluded from the classification task because their SCP labels map to none of the 5 diagnostic superclasses, leaving the 21,388-record standard PTB-XL superdiagnostic subset (this is a label-coverage filter, not a pacemaker filter; 291 records carry a pacemaker annotation and most, but not all, fall inside these 411)
  • Official split used throughout, exactly as the dataset paper prescribes: folds 1 to 8 for training, fold 9 for validation, fold 10 for testing. Splits are patient-disjoint (the same patient never appears in both train and test), which prevents the model from memorizing a person instead of learning a pattern

The method

Step 1. Reproduce the official baselines. Using the official benchmarking code (helme/ecg_ptbxl_benchmarking, Strodthoff et al. 2020, arXiv:2004.13701), two 12-channel models were trained on the exact official split:

ModelTest AUC
resnet1d_wang0.9293
inception1d0.9219

These match the published benchmark numbers, confirming the pipeline is correct before any new idea is tested.

Step 2. The VCG transform. The Kors 1990 regression (Kors et al. 1990, European Heart Journal) computes 3 channels (X, Y, Z = the 3D cardiac vector) from 8 of the 12 leads, using 24 published coefficients. All 24 coefficients were independently cross-verified against a second open-access source (Jaros et al. 2019, Sensors, Table 2) and agree exactly. The VCG model simply gets those 3 extra channels alongside the 12 raw leads (15 channels total).

Step 3. The controlled A/B test (Phase B). Same architecture, hyperparameters, seed value (42) and train/val/test split. Only the input channels differ: 12 (control) vs 15 (VCG-augmented). Because the first layer then has a different number of weights, the same seed does not give matched initialisation, and each arm was trained once, so the intervals below reflect test-set sampling only, not run-to-run variation.

The results

Finding 1: Phase B null result (the main test)

RunChannelsTest AUC90% CI
Control120.9301[0.9240, 0.9348]
VCG-augmented150.9305[0.9247, 0.9355]

Paired difference (VCG minus control) = +0.00042 on the full test set. Over 100 paired bootstrap resamples the mean is +0.00055 and the 95% interval is [−0.00216, +0.00305]. The interval crosses zero: no statistically significant difference. The physics channels add no measurable diagnostic value on top of the raw leads under this architecture and protocol. That fits what they are: a fixed linear combination of leads the network already receives.

Finding 2: the Einthoven-consistency audit (standalone, citable)

Because the 6 limb leads obey exact arithmetic identities (Einthoven's law: II = I + III, and Goldberger's equations for the augmented leads), every record can be checked for internal consistency. We ran this check on all 21,799 PTB-XL records in the 100 Hz files:

  • 99.74% of records are consistent to within the 5 microvolt tolerance (99.61% to within 1.5 microvolts). The leads are stored as whole microvolts, so Einthoven's residual takes whole-microvolt values and the augmented residuals multiples of 0.5 microvolts. Typical maxima are 1 to 1.5 microvolts; augmented maxima of 1.5 microvolts exceed what a single rounding produces, so the leads were probably rounded at more than one processing stage
  • Only 57 records (0.26%) are flagged (an identity off by more than 5 microvolts). In 34 of them every break falls in the last 0.1 s of the recording; the longest continuous break lasts 0.33 s
  • Flags concentrate in paced recordings: 11 of the 57 are paced, against 291 of all 21,799 records (odds ratio 18)
  • PTB-XL's own built-in noise annotations show no detectable association with the audit flag (0.20% of annotated records flagged vs 0.28% of unannotated records; odds ratio 0.71, 95% interval 0.32 to 1.43, p = 0.43)

This is a standalone quality-control result for anyone using PTB-XL: the audit dataset and the verified transform coefficients are released in the repository.

Finding 3: the 500 Hz files and a cross-rate check (paper in preparation)

The same audit run on PTB-XL's 500 Hz files, and a comparison of the two file sets:

  • At 500 Hz only 9 records are flagged, and just 3 of them are among the 57 flagged at 100 Hz
  • Every 500 Hz file ends with its last 45 to 65 samples (about 0.1 s) held constant in all 12 leads. All breaks of the 34 end-of-recording records at 100 Hz fall inside this held end, so the two file sets cannot be compared there
  • Away from the recording ends, one fixed linear filter maps the 500 Hz files onto the 100 Hz files to within 1 microvolt in 21,685 of the 21,736 records unflagged at both rates, so each file set can check the other, lead by lead
  • That check shows which leads depart at the 100 Hz breaks, and it exposes 17 records that pass the identities at both rates but whose chest leads differ between the file sets by 1.1 to 43.8 mV, faults the identities cannot see
  • Removing the 4 test records flagged at 100 Hz (the files the models use) changes test macro AUC by less than 0.0002. The flagged training records were kept, and the effect of the other faults on the benchmark was not tested

The files cannot show which processing step produced each fault.

Finding 4: do the residuals carry diagnostic info? (Spec 05)

Follow-up question: the consistency checks leave a small residual (how far a record deviates from the exact identity). Could that residual differ by diagnosis? Tested on the 21,508 non-paced records, using the exact residual maxima of the 100 Hz files, with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (alpha 0.05) applied separately to the 4 Kruskal-Wallis tests and to the 20 Mann-Whitney tests:

  • 4 Kruskal-Wallis tests on the 16,235 single-label records (one per identity): 2 survive correction (aVR and aVL), and the largest effect size is eta-squared = 0.0011, far below the 0.01 "small effect" threshold. Einthoven's residual shows no detectable difference (H = 2.3, p = 0.68)
  • 20 Mann-Whitney tests on 21,374 records (4 identities x 5 classes, each class against the rest): 5 survive correction (for aVR, aVL and aVF), and the largest |Cliff's delta| is 0.026, well below the 0.147 "negligible" boundary
  • No superclass has a flag rate that differs detectably from that of the other non-paced records (odds ratios 0.71 to 1.50, p = 0.26 to 0.86)

Conclusion: a few tests survive correction, but every effect is negligible. In non-paced records, the residual maxima do not separate the five diagnostic superclasses in any useful way. An earlier version of this page reported 3 of 4 and 8 of 20 surviving tests and larger effect sizes. The spurious survivors, all for Einthoven's residual (one Kruskal-Wallis test and four Mann-Whitney tests), and the larger effects came from floating-point noise in the residual values (below 10−12 microvolts), which rank-based tests treat as real differences between values that are equal. With exact values one test that did not survive before (aVF, CD against the rest) now does.

Withdrawn: cross-dataset test on CPSC2018 (Spec 06)

An earlier version of this page reported macro AUCs of 0.78 to 0.85 for the PTB-XL model run, without retraining, on CPSC2018. A later check found that the evaluation did not match the trained model's configuration (its stride settings), input normalisation or windowing, so those numbers do not measure the model and are withdrawn. The test has not been re-run.

What the results mean

Three honest takeaways:

  1. The main hypothesis did not survive the test. For resnet1d_wang on PTB-XL, the 3 Kors-reconstructed VCG channels are redundant: they are linear combinations of leads the model already receives, and adding them did not measurably change performance (one training run per arm). This is a negative result reported as a negative result, not papered over.
  2. The negative result is informative, not worthless. It narrows where reconstructed VCG inputs could still help: architectures that cannot learn lead relationships as well, smaller datasets, or other tasks. Measured Frank leads, which come from extra electrodes, were not tested.
  3. The side findings are real contributions. (a) A consistency audit for PTB-XL at both sampling rates (the 100 Hz data are released now, the 500 Hz data will follow with the paper), with verified Kors coefficients, (b) a cross-rate check that lets PTB-XL's two file sets test each other lead by lead and exposes chest-lead faults the identities cannot see, (c) an FDR-controlled analysis showing that, in non-paced records, the residual maxima differ only negligibly between the five diagnostic superclasses.
